01 · About

From the neuroscience lab to learning design

I started my career studying how the brain works. That research background in neuroscience and neurochemistry now shapes everything I design. I translate what we know about attention, memory and motivation into learning that people actually finish, enjoy and remember.

Today I partner with academics, clinicians, subject-matter experts and cross-functional teams to design user-centred, interactive programs. I also redesign assessment so that it is authentic, sustainable and aligned with standards. My toolkit spans instructional and curriculum design, UX research, gamification, animation and multimedia, and practical AI integration.

  • Evidence first

    I ground each design in learning science, including cognitive load, retrieval practice and feedback.

  • Learner-centred UX

    I apply UX research and accessibility from the first sketch to the final build.

  • Play with purpose

    My games and challenges build motivation and mastery, not just points.

  • AI, responsibly

    I use AI to personalise learning and help teams adopt it with confidence.

You just experienced my design approach

Those six quests weren't just decoration. They quietly used the same principles I build into real programs:

  • Retrieval practice: answering a question strengthens memory more than rereading.
  • Immediate, explanatory feedback: every answer told you why, not just right or wrong.
  • Safe failure: you could retry with no penalty, which keeps you motivated.
  • Autonomy & visible progress: you chose the order, and the XP bar showed your competence growing.
  • Chunking: short, focused challenges kept the cognitive load low.
